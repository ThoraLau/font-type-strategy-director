# Specimen and poster protocol

The visual is a strategy test, not decoration.

## Required content layers

Use the user's actual or representative content:

- brand or project name;
- one decisive headline;
- short value proposition;
- body paragraph;
- functional line such as price, date, navigation, specification, or CTA;
- numerals and punctuation when relevant;
- font family, styles, and role labels outside the artwork when appropriate.

## Compare fairly

For candidate comparison:

- keep copy, canvas, color, and image constant initially;
- adjust size, leading, and tracking for each font's actual proportions;
- show both a controlled comparison and one optimized expression;
- do not make the preferred candidate win through a better layout;
- test at realistic mobile/desktop or print sizes.

## Art direction

The composition should demonstrate the strategy's cultural and temporal logic through hierarchy, density, scale, rhythm, and imagery. Do not reproduce a designer's famous poster, a foundry specimen, or a living brand identity.

## Font-file and license rules

- Open-license font: keep the license and attribution information; verify redistribution and modification conditions.
- Commercial font supplied by the user: use it only for the authorized task and do not package or redistribute it in the Skill.
- Commercial font not supplied: create a wireframe/art-direction specification and link to the official specimen.
- Unknown license: do not download, embed, or render it.
- A fallback font must be labeled as a fallback, never as the recommended commercial family.

## Delivery

Provide one of:

- a PNG poster with exact font provenance;
- a comparison sheet for 3-5 candidates;
- an HTML specimen using legally accessible webfonts;
- an art-direction board when licensed files are unavailable.

Include a concise note explaining which parts demonstrate the intended semiotic signals and which remain hypotheses for audience testing.
